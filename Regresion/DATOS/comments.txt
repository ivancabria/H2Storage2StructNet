Usable vc vs usable gc
There is a relationship between usable vc and usable gc:

The usable volumetric capacity is uvc = MuH/V
The usable gravimetric capacity is ugc = 100 MuH/(MuH + Madsorbent)

MuH: usable mass of hydrogen
Madsorbent: mass of the adsorbent material. In this case, the mass of the 
MOF.
These are the masses in the simulation cell.
V: volume of the simulation cell

If Muc is low (this is the case for hydrogen) then, MuH << Madsorbent 
and the following approach is valid:

ugc = 100 MuH/(MuH + Madsorbent) approx 100 MuH/Madsorbent = 
= 100 (MuH/V) / (Madsorbent/V) = 100 uvc/density

The density and the ssa are correlated. SSA decreases exponentially as the 
density increases. See figure 8 in graph.pdf. According to the plot of the 
SSA vs density, SSA values of 7000 or 14000 m^2/g should be with densities 
<= 0.5 kg/L.

The density and the specific pore volume are correlated. The specific pore 
volume decreases exponentially as the density increases. See figure 9 in 
graph.pdf. According to the plot of the specific pore volume vs density, 
specific pore volumes of 1 cm^3/g should be with densities of approximately 
0.5 kg/L.

The density and the largest pore radius, Ri, are correlated. The largest pore 
radius decreases exponentially as the density increases. See figure 10 in 
graph.pdf. According to the plot of the largest pore radius vs density, 
densities of 1.8 or 2.8 kg/L are consistent with Ri approx. 3 angstroms, 
and densities <=0.6 kg/L correspond to Ri >= 4 angstroms.
